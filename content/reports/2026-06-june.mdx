<SectionHeading number="01">Headline Finding</SectionHeading>

The June reading across Ethereum's six largest lending protocols is consolidation under yield compression at the sector level. The Real Yield Spread between blended stablecoin lending and the 4-week U.S. Treasury bill closed June 30 at −37.1 basis points, a 36.8-basis-point deepening from the May 31 print and the deepest month-end inversion since March 2026. Capital did not leave the sector in response. The six protocols absorbed $1.33 billion of net deposits at constant prices, with Aave V3's $845 million the largest single-protocol inflow in the captured series and Spark's $415 million a distant second. Aave V3's $845 million did not arrive in USDC. Its own USDC book shed $162 million of net constant-price capital across the month. What arrived was $452 million of wstETH, $143 million of cbBTC, $104 million of USDTB, $99 million of USDT, and smaller positives in USDE, SUSDE, WBTC, WETH, and WEETH. Aave V3's headline USDC supply APY at 3.19 percent ran 41 basis points below the 4-week Treasury bill through the month, but the depositor flow that landed on the protocol arrived in ETH-family and BTC-family collateral and in stablecoin alternatives to USDC. Fluid, whose USDC supply APY at 6.41 percent ran 281 basis points above the T-bill and was the sector's only positive real yield spread on USDC, received $16 million of net inflow. Five of the six protocols saw positive flow. The exception was Euler V2, and its $21 million outflow was operational rather than depositor-driven. The paradox the June flow reveals is not that depositors chose USDC on Aave V3 over USDC on Fluid; USDC on Aave V3 actually contracted. It is that Aave V3 absorbed 63 percent of sector-wide net inflow while sector stablecoin rates sat below the T-bill, on assets other than the one whose rate defined the T-bill comparison. Depositors were not rewarded for choosing the largest venue; they chose it anyway, and the assets they added were the ones the rate compression did not touch. Issue 002's rotation thesis terminates at the sector layer. The June reading is that when sector yields compress below the risk-free rate, capital does not chase the marginal yield elsewhere. It consolidates into the largest existing books. Whether the same mechanism runs at the curator level inside Morpho is a separate question the Morpho deep dive later in this issue examines, and the answer, once measured across both of Morpho's vault contract systems, is different from what the Issue 002 curator-concentration reading would suggest.

<SectionHeading number="03">The Real Yield Spread, Deep Inversion</SectionHeading>

<Lead>The Real Yield Spread closed June 2026 at −37.1 basis points, a 36.8-basis-point deepening from May 31 and the deepest month-end inversion since March 2026. May's parity reading lasted one calendar month.</Lead>

The mechanical decomposition is the inverse of May's. Stable APYs fell while T-bill yields held flat. The blended stablecoin supply rate, TVL-weighted across USDC, USDT, DAI, and USDS on Aave V3, Spark, Morpho, and Fluid, fell from 3.60 percent at the May close to 3.23 percent at the June close, a 37-basis-point compression. The 4-week U.S. Treasury bill yield held at 3.60 percent across the same window, unchanged to two decimal places. The spread re-inverted because on-chain rates fell, not because TradFi rates moved. That is the inverse of May's mechanism, where stable APYs lifted to meet T-bills.

What June added to the picture is duration of inversion. The Real Yield Spread last printed positive on June 4, 2026, at +42.0 basis points. Through June 30, that prior positive print sits 25 days back, and the intervening series has been continuously negative. The June 4 reading was a one-day blip; the run before it was already negative. The functional sequence within the month is a brief positive print on day four, followed by a deepening inversion through the remaining 26 days.

<PullQuote>May's parity reading lasted one calendar month.</PullQuote>

Against the captured historical series, June's −37 basis points is not the deepest reading on record. The 13-month trajectory has the deepest inversion at February 2026's −151 basis points, with March 2026 at −118 basis points and January 2026 at −64 basis points, all deeper than June. April 2026 lifted to −28 basis points and May closed at parity before June re-inverted. Against the prior cycle, the May to November 2025 month-end series ranged from August 2025's −19 basis points to June 2025's −90 basis points, with two positive prints in between (July 2025 +26 basis points, September 2025 +38 basis points). June 2026's reading places this month near the mid-point of the prior cycle's inverted regime: deeper than October 2025's −41 basis points, shallower than November 2025's −63 basis points. June's print is consistent with the spread reverting to the pre-rally regime, rather than continuing the recovery that May suggested.

The interesting tension is what depositor capital did in response. Aave V3 absorbed $845 million of net constant-price deposits across June, the largest captured single-month inflow at the protocol level. The Aave V3 USDC supply APY at June 30 closed at 3.19 percent, against the 3.60 percent T-bill yield, an effective 41-basis-point penalty for holding stables on the largest Ethereum lending protocol versus the U.S. government's short-dated debt. The paradox does not run through USDC itself. Aave V3's USDC book actually shed $162 million of net constant-price capital across June. The $845 million protocol-level inflow arrived in wstETH (+$452 million), cbBTC (+$143 million), USDTB (+$104 million), USDT (+$99 million), and smaller positives in USDE, SUSDE, WBTC, WETH, and WEETH, none of them the flagship stablecoin whose headline rate defined the T-bill penalty. Depositors chose the largest protocol on ETH-family and BTC-family collateral and on stablecoin alternatives to USDC, while the USDC book itself contracted. Fluid, whose USDC supply APY rose 48 basis points in June to 6.41 percent, 281 basis points above the T-bill and the only positive real yield spread among the four USDC-market venues, received $16 million of net inflow. That 50-to-1 protocol-level flow ratio to Aave V3 is not a 50-to-1 USDC-vs-USDC comparison; it is a comparison of two protocols where one grew on non-USDC assets and the other received a small positive USDC-specific inflow at the sector's highest USDC rate. The Fluid deep dive later in this issue unpacks Fluid's rate divergence in detail, and the Aave V3 deep dive picks this up at the asset level.

June 5, 2026 marked the month's largest liquidation event across the sector. Sector-wide collateral seized on the day reached $128.31 million across 1,766 individual events, ranking first among the month's thirty trading days and running 10.25 times the trailing seven-day median of $12.51 million. Aave V3 carried $93.98 million of the day's seized collateral, Fluid $15.04 million, Morpho $11.08 million, Compound V3 $7.22 million, and SparkLend $964 thousand, with Euler V2's residual $21 thousand rounding out the day. The multi-protocol distribution indicates a spot-price shock that traveled across the sector rather than a single-protocol operational failure. Alongside the mark-to-market compression on LRT and ETH-family collateral, the June 5 event is one of the two proximate mechanical drivers of the sector-wide $3.41 billion nominal-supply contraction across the month, and it lands squarely in the middle of the yield-compression window this section tracks.

A candidate parallel would sit inside Morpho's curator concentration. Issue 002 documented a Morpho curator HHI of 3,103 at May 31 with the top three curators holding 93.9 percent of curated total value locked, using a manual dashboard capture. That reading was drawn from a query hitting Morpho's V1 vault contract system only. When Morpho's V1 and V2 vault contract systems are aggregated together at June 30, the combined HHI is 2,095 with the top-three share at 74.6 percent. Sentora is the largest curator at 31.2 percent of combined curated total value locked, primarily via V2 flagship vaults that have been operational since March. The Morpho deep dive later in this issue unpacks the methodological correction in full.

Whether July sees the inversion persist or close depends primarily on whether T-bill yields fall to meet on-chain rates, or on-chain rates lift to meet T-bills. The next Federal Open Market Committee decision lands in late July; absent a cut, the prior cycle suggests the inversion holds near current levels.

The distribution shape distinguishes June's inflow from Issue 002's May reading. May's flow pattern was rotation: Aave V3 shed $503 million while SparkLend and Morpho absorbed nearly identical shares of the exit, roughly $752 million each. Neither absorbing protocol pulled clear ahead of the other. Depositors leaving Aave V3 in May chose between two migration destinations that ran nearly equal in the outcome data. June inverted that pattern in every direction. Aave V3 was the largest inflow at $845 million, not the largest outflow. SparkLend continued as a beneficiary but at half of May's rate. Every protocol except Euler V2 saw net positive flow, and the largest recipient was also the largest existing protocol by supply. The rotation thesis Issue 002 built out has been replaced by a consolidation pattern in which the sector's biggest venue absorbed the majority of new capital.

Aave V3 captured 63.4 percent of the sector's net constant-price inflow while holding 56.7 percent of the sector's nominal supply at June 30. Its share of inflow ran roughly seven percentage points ahead of its share of stock. SparkLend captured 31.1 percent of inflow against a 17.2 percent share of supply, running an even more pronounced over-index at 14 points above its stock share. The remaining four protocols together captured 5.5 percent of inflow while holding 26.1 percent of supply, materially under-indexing on flow relative to stock. The month's flow accelerated the concentration that already existed at the protocol layer, and it did so during a month where the on-chain yield on the flow's largest destination sat 41 basis points below the risk-free rate.

Three of June's four material single-day net outflows on Aave V3 were intra-sector or intra-protocol asset rotations rather than depositor exits. The June 12 outflow of $89.5 million was dominated by USDC leaving Aave V3 alongside wstETH inflows on the same day. June 11's $85.6 million outflow ran the same shape. Only June 3's $70.1 million outflow carried genuine cross-asset departure at any material scale. Sector-wide, the flow signal is that depositors added quantity on twenty-seven of thirty June trading days across Aave V3 alone, and on aggregate positive-flow days across every other protocol except Euler V2. The Aave V3 deep dive picks up the paradox of the protocol's inflow against yield compression. The SparkLend deep dive picks up the SparkLend read as the second-tier destination. The Euler V2 deep dive unpacks Euler V2's small outflow as broad residual movement rather than a single operator event. The Fluid and Compound V3 deep dives note the smaller positives at those two protocols. The Morpho deep dive handles the Morpho story separately: the protocol saw only +$20 million of constant-price flow in June, but the more important Morpho finding is a methodological correction to Issue 002's curator-concentration reading, which the section documents in full.

<SectionHeading number="05">The LRT Correction</SectionHeading>

<Lead>Issue 002's LRT section framed May's LRT contraction as depositor exit. The sector lost $1.62 billion of LRT collateral at actual prices across May, of which $1.17 billion was constant-price outflow and $443 million was price decline. June extended the contraction at the actual-price level but inverted the mechanism. June's LRT collateral fell almost entirely because collateral repriced, not because depositors withdrew.</Lead>

The four largest liquid restaking tokens fell in near-identical proportions in June. weETH fell 21.04 percent per token, from $2,193.22 at May 31 to $1,731.73 at June 30. rsETH fell 21.00 percent. ezETH fell 21.28 percent. osETH fell 19.99 percent. Spot ETH fell 21.25 percent across the same window, from $2,004.62 to $1,578.55. The four largest LRTs moved almost mechanically together with ETH, which is what the collateral base of a well-collateralized LST wrapper should do. The mechanism is bond math on the underlying stake: when ETH falls twenty-one percent, wrapped stake positions fall by roughly the same percentage after accrual, and any LRT running a small basis premium moves within a percentage point of ETH's direct move.

The Aave V3 weETH book anchors the arithmetic. At May 31, weETH on Aave V3 Core carried $2.11 billion of supply. At June 30, $1.69 billion. That is a $419 million nominal contraction on one asset in one market. Priced against the May 31 spot of $2,193.22, the May 31 balance implied roughly 961,000 weETH tokens sitting on the market. Priced against the June 30 spot of $1,731.73, the June 30 balance implied roughly 975,000 weETH tokens. Depositor quantity on Aave V3 Core's weETH book grew by approximately 14,000 tokens across June, worth about $34 million at the June 30 price. The remaining $453 million of the nominal contraction is entirely per-unit price effect. Aave V3 Prime and Aave V3 Horizon carried no weETH at either endpoint, so the Core read is the full Aave V3 read.

<PullQuote>Aave V3 Core weETH depositors added roughly 14,000 tokens across June while the position's dollar value fell $419 million.</PullQuote>

Extended across the sector, the June LRT picture is a $3.73 billion sector-wide LRT collateral position at May 31 versus $2.92 billion at June 30, an $807 million actual-price contraction across the four largest LRTs. Priced at June 30 spot rates against May 31 token quantities, the per-unit repricing on weETH, rsETH, ezETH, and osETH accounts for roughly $781 million of the $807 million move. The residual, approximately $26 million, is the constant-price depositor flow component across the four LRTs combined. Depositors added weETH quantity modestly, removed rsETH and ezETH quantity in smaller absolute magnitudes, and held osETH roughly flat. That $26 million net-out figure is a fraction of May's $1.17 billion constant-price LRT outflow. June's contraction is roughly 97 percent price effect and 3 percent depositor flow.

The May thesis Issue 002's LRT section built out held rigorously for May. Depositors exited LRT collateral in real quantity across the month, with $1.17 billion of constant-price outflow that was a genuine behavioral shift concentrated on weETH. June did not repeat that pattern. The Aave V3 weETH position, which absorbed most of May's outflow, saw depositors ADD tokens in June. Depositors who stayed through May stayed through June, and depositors on the Aave V3 Core book added modestly to their positions across the month even as the dollar value of what they held fell substantially. The post-May-14 LRT-exit thesis Issue 002's LRT section documented remains correct for May. The mechanism did not extend to June.

The forward question sits at the intersection of LRT depositor behavior and ETH price direction. If July's LRT spot prices stabilize or recover, the depositors who held through the two-month drawdown will be sitting on positions worth substantially more in dollar terms without having added or withdrawn. If prices continue declining at June's rate, the same depositors will see another 20 percent haircut on the dollar value of unchanged collateral. Whether that second haircut catalyzes exit at the magnitude May absorbed is not visible in the captured LRT data alone. The signal that would answer the question is a July constant-price flow reading against a further LRT price decline, which the sector overview will pick up when the next month's data lands.

<SectionHeading number="06.1">Protocol Deep Dive: Aave V3</SectionHeading>

The canonical multi-asset lending pool. Shared liquidity, isolation mode for risky assets, E-mode for correlated pairs.

<Lead>Aave V3 closed June 30 with USDC supply APY at 3.19 percent against a 4-week U.S. Treasury bill yield of 3.60 percent. Depositors accepted a 41-basis-point penalty per dollar supplied and added $845 million of net constant-price deposits across the month. That is the largest single-protocol inflow in the captured series, and it arrived while the marginal dollar was compensated less on Aave V3 than in short-dated Treasuries.</Lead>

At June 30, Aave V3 carried $16.59 billion of total supply on Ethereum against $7.26 billion of active borrows, a loan-to-deposit ratio of 43.8 percent. The nominal supply fell $2.02 billion from May 31, a 10.9 percent contraction; the constant-price series lifted $845 million across the same window. The gap of roughly $2.87 billion is mark-to-market on collateral, dominated by the 21.04 percent per-unit decline in weETH and the 21.25 percent decline in spot ETH between May 31 and June 30. Both readings are correct at their own definitions. The dollar value of the depositor stake shrank; the underlying depositor quantity grew.

The asset-level composition of the growth is not uniform. Three days in June printed material net outflows: June 3 at −$70 million, June 11 at −$86 million, June 12 at −$90 million. Across those three days USDC accounted for approximately $156 million of gross outflow, USDT $18 million, and WETH $16 million. Inflows on the same days concentrated in wstETH ($98 million across the three days), cbBTC ($20 million), and USDtb ($19 million). What ran off Aave V3 on the days when anything ran off at all was stablecoins. What replaced it was a mix of staked-ETH, wrapped-BTC variants, and a small stablecoin with a specific carry story. On the twenty-seven other days in June, the net was cumulatively about +$1,090 million and broadly distributed. The month's inflow was not a single trade at scale; it was steady accumulation punctuated by three days of stable-token rotation.

<PullQuote>Depositors accepted a 41-basis-point penalty and added $845 million of net constant-price deposits anyway.</PullQuote>

Aave V3's weETH book is the sharpest example of the accounting wedge. At May 31, weETH on Aave V3 Core stood at $2.11 billion; at June 30, $1.69 billion. The $419 million nominal decline is dominated by price: at the June 30 spot of $1,731.73 per token, the roughly 961,000-weETH position implied by the May 31 stake carried approximately $450 million less USD value than at May 31's spot of $2,193.22. The remainder, once the price component is stripped out, is a small net inflow of approximately $34 million of depositor quantity across the month. Aave V3 Core weETH depositors did not exit in June. They added a modest position while the price cratered. This reverses May's LRT-exit trajectory documented in Issue 002's LRT section, at least at the Aave V3 Core boundary. Aave V3 Prime and Aave V3 Horizon carried no weETH at either endpoint. The full Aave V3 weETH read for June is a single-market read.

Two candidate mechanisms explain why depositors accepted the rate penalty. The first is depth. Aave V3's supply book at $16.59 billion is roughly 3.3 times the size of the second-largest protocol in coverage, SparkLend at $5.03 billion, and 3.3 times the size of Morpho at $5.09 billion. When sector yields compress and the marginal dollar is choosing between protocols primarily on redemption depth rather than headline rate, the largest pool retains the flow by default. The second is protocol-specific carry structure. Aave V3 hosts specific collateral relationships (E-mode pairs, isolation-mode positions, LRT-loop residues) that do not exist in the same configuration on other protocols. Depositors already inside those positions face non-trivial migration costs even if a headline stablecoin APY is 100 basis points higher elsewhere. Neither mechanism resolves cleanly from June's data alone. The distinction between "depth as inertia" and "depth as active preference" is what next month's flow data can begin to disambiguate.

Aave V3's June inflow is the largest single-protocol expression of a sector-wide consolidation, not a story of Aave V3 growing at other protocols' expense. Sparkland added $415 million of net constant-price flow across June; Compound V3, Morpho, and Fluid each added smaller positives; only Euler V2 saw net outflow, and its contraction was operational rather than depositor-driven. The sector added $1.33 billion in aggregate across six protocols, and Aave V3 captured 63 percent of that total by virtue of already holding 57 percent of sector supply. The consolidation is protocol-level and sector-level simultaneously. Aave V3 leads by size rather than by being the only game in town. The Morpho deep dive picks up the same consolidation logic playing out one level down, at the curator layer inside Morpho.

<SectionHeading number="06.2">Protocol Deep Dive: Morpho</SectionHeading>

Permissionless isolated lending primitives. Each market is a single tuple of loan asset, collateral, LLTV, oracle, and IRM. Vaults aggregate across markets, and curators manage the vaults. As of June 2026, Morpho operates two vault contract systems in parallel: MetaMorpho (referred to as V1) and Vault V2, which launched earlier in the year.

<Lead>Morpho's curator concentration at June 30, 2026 is less concentrated than Issue 002 reported for May 31, not more. The correct combined view across both vault contract systems gives an HHI of 2,095 and a top-three curator share of 74.6 percent. Issue 002's May 31 reading of HHI 3,103 and top-three share 93.9 percent captured only V1. V2 was already the larger side of the curator market at May 31 and has grown further since.</Lead>

At June 30, Morpho carried $5.09 billion of nominal supply on Ethereum against $1.84 billion of active borrows, a loan-to-deposit ratio of 36.1 percent. Constant-price net flow across the month was positive $20.3 million. The $870 million nominal contraction is roughly $890 million of mark-to-market on Morpho's LRT-heavy curator vault collateral, mirroring the pattern the Aave V3 deep dive documents for its weETH book. Depositors did not leave Morpho in June; the collateral they hold repriced.

The finding that requires correction is the curator layer. Morpho's curator market at June 30, measured across both V1 and V2 vault contract systems, holds $2.12 billion of curated total value locked. V1 accounts for $672.56 million and V2 for $1.59 billion. V2 is 2.4 times the size of V1 at this snapshot, and V2 has been operational and accumulating capital since at least March 2026.

Twenty curators share that $2.12 billion. Sentora ranks first at 31.2 percent, or $661.76 million, all of it in V2 across three flagship vaults (Sentora RLUSD Main deployed 2026-03-04, Sentora PRIME Main deployed 2026-05-08, and a PayPal-branded PYUSD vault Sentora curates on the same infrastructure). Steakhouse Financial ranks second at 29.2 percent, or $618.15 million, split roughly evenly across V1 ($316 million on the long-standing 0xBEEF and 0xbeef vanity vault addresses) and V2 ($302 million). Gauntlet ranks third at 14.2 percent, or $300.83 million, still V1-heavy. The remaining 25.0 percent distributes across seventeen curators including Sky Money, Galaxy Curation, SparkDAO, Armitage by Wintermute, Waterline, AlphaPing, Clearstar, and others, none holding more than 5.3 percent individually.

<PullQuote>The concentration story Issue 002 told holds at the V1 layer. It does not translate to the protocol layer once V2 is included.</PullQuote>

Issue 002's May 31 curator reading was a manual capture of the dashboard's HHI panel, which sourced its data from the V1 GraphQL type on Morpho's Blue API. That reading recorded three named curators (Sentora, Steakhouse, Gauntlet) at 93.9 percent combined share and an HHI of 3,103. The V1-only reading was accurate on its own terms. It answered the question "how concentrated is Morpho V1 among curators of V1 vaults?" It did not answer the question the prose framed it as, which was "how concentrated is Morpho's curator layer as a whole?" On the second question, at May 31, V2 was already the larger side of the market, and by June 30 the combined-view answer is 2,095, or roughly the ordinary-competitive band for a curator ecosystem with a distinct leader and a healthy second tier.

The correction has one editorial consequence and one methodological consequence.

The editorial consequence is that the curator-layer analog to the sector-level consolidation that the headline documents does not hold on the correct data. The Aave V3 deep dive shows Aave V3 absorbing 63 percent of net constant-price inflow at the protocol level by virtue of already holding 57 percent of sector supply. The analogous mechanism at the curator level inside Morpho would have Sentora or Steakhouse absorbing the majority of Morpho's curator-level flows through June. That is not what the combined view shows. Sentora's V2 book, which was already large at May 31, moved by a comparable share of V2 flows in June to what other established V2 curators moved. Steakhouse's V2 book grew alongside. The curator market at Morpho did not concentrate further in June; the discovery is that it was less concentrated than we thought all along, because half the market was invisible to the reading Issue 002 used.

The methodological consequence is that Morpho concentration metrics require the combined V1+V2 view going forward. The historical time series persisted in Neon's `morpho_curator_hhi_history` table is a V1-only series through this snapshot date; the V1+V2 combined series begins at July 2, 2026, and the two are not directly comparable. A rebuild of the V1+V2 historical series is possible via per-vault historical state queries on Blue API. That rebuild is out of scope for Issue 003 and is documented as a follow-up in the source file for this section.

What the June 30 reading does support at the protocol level is that Morpho's book grew in the way flexible-curator lending would predict when sector yields compressed. Depositors did not run to Steakhouse specifically or away from Sentora specifically. Both grew their books. Smaller curators grew alongside them. The curator ecosystem behaves like a competitive market with distinct operators serving distinct depositor bases, which is a different structural read from the near-monopoly the V1-only reading suggested. The Aave V3 deep dive documents the protocol-level consolidation; this Morpho deep dive documents that the curator-level version of the same claim does not hold when measured correctly.

<SectionHeading number="07">Looking Ahead</SectionHeading>

The June data supports six forward calls for July. Each is stated with the mechanism it depends on and the July-data reading that would discriminate cleanly. Issue 004 can honestly correct any call that misses.

The first call sits on the Real Yield Spread. At the July 31 close, expect the spread to land somewhere between −20 and −60 basis points. The mechanism is that T-bill yields likely hold at 3.60 percent through the Federal Open Market Committee's late-July meeting absent a surprise cut, while on-chain rate compression persists at the current sector deposit trajectory. If the FOMC cuts, expect the spread to close from the rate side (T-bill down, on-chain rates roughly flat) rather than from the on-chain side. The discrimination is the July 31 spread print combined with the actual FOMC decision.

The second call is Aave V3's dominance of sector inflow. Expect Aave V3's share of net constant-price sector inflow in July to remain above 50 percent, most likely in the 55 to 70 percent range. The mechanism is the trust-over-yield thesis the headline documents: if the Real Yield Spread stays inverted, depositors continue accepting the yield penalty in exchange for redemption depth at the largest venue. Fluid's June rate leadership at 6.41 percent USDC could pull marginal share, but the 50-to-1 Aave-to-Fluid June flow ratio suggests the pull will not be structural. Discrimination sits in July daily-flow CSVs across all six covered protocols.

The third call is Fluid's rate divergence persistence. Expect Fluid USDC supply APY to stay above 5 percent through July, and Fluid's constant-price net inflow to grow modestly but stay under $50 million for the month. The mechanism is that Fluid's USDC rate is a function of borrow demand on its lending markets, which have been growing through the second quarter of 2026 per the LDR trajectory documented across Issues 001 and 002. Depositor response to the rate stays small because the June 50-to-1 ratio suggests trust-over-yield is a real preference rather than a one-month artifact. Discrimination sits in the July Aave V3 weETH-by-market snapshot with the quantity column, not the dollar column.

The sixth call is inside Morpho's curator layer. Expect Steakhouse Financial to grow its combined V1+V2 share from 29.2 percent at June 30 toward 30 to 35 percent by July 31. Expect Sentora to hold or grow their V2 share from the current 31.2 percent. The consolidation-under-yield-compression thesis Issue 003 documents is falsifiable through July's data. If Aave V3's share of sector inflow falls below 50 percent and Fluid's constant-price flow rises above 75 million dollars, the trust-over-yield thesis needs revision. If the Real Yield Spread closes from the rate side while flow patterns hold, June's consolidation was cyclical rather than structural. The Issue 003 reading is that both mechanisms are structural, and the July close provides the discriminating datapoint that either confirms the reading or requires the correction Issue 004 will publish.
