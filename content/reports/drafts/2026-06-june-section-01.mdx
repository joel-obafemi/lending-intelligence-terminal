<SectionHeading number="01">Headline Finding</SectionHeading>

The June reading across Ethereum's six largest lending protocols is consolidation under yield compression at the sector level. The Real Yield Spread between blended stablecoin lending and the 4-week U.S. Treasury bill closed June 30 at −37.1 basis points, a 36.8-basis-point deepening from the May 31 print and the deepest month-end inversion since March 2026. Capital did not leave the sector in response. The six protocols absorbed $1.33 billion of net deposits at constant prices, with Aave V3's $845 million the largest single-protocol inflow in the captured series and Spark's $415 million a distant second. Depositors chose Aave V3 at 3.19 percent USDC over Fluid at 6.41 percent by roughly 50 to 1 in June flow, even though Fluid's rate ran 281 basis points above the 4-week Treasury bill and Aave V3's ran 41 basis points below. Five of the six saw positive flow. The exception was Euler V2, and its $21 million outflow was operational rather than depositor-driven. §06.1 unpacks the paradox at the protocol level: Aave V3 absorbed 63 percent of sector-wide net inflow while its USDC supply rate sat below the T-bill. Depositors were not rewarded for choosing the largest venue; they chose it anyway. Issue 002's rotation thesis terminates at the sector layer. The June reading is that when sector yields compress below the risk-free rate, capital does not chase the marginal yield elsewhere. It consolidates into the largest existing books. Whether the same mechanism runs at the curator level inside Morpho is a separate question §06.2 examines, and the answer, once measured across both of Morpho's vault contract systems, is different from what the Issue 002 curator-concentration reading would suggest.
