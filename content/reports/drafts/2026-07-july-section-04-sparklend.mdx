<SectionHeading number="04" subsection="SparkLend" title="SparkLend" />

SparkLend's July constant-price net deposit flow was −$203M at the protocol level. That is the largest single-protocol contraction of the month across the six covered protocols. Available pool liquidity at July 31: $3.76B. Borrow book: $1.81B. Loan-to-deposit ratio: 32.53%, below the 40.63% sector aggregate.

The month contained two outsized single-day outflows that shape the total: July 12 (−$315M) and July 28 (−$173M). The other 29 days combined for approximately +$285M of offsetting inflows. The July 28 outflow followed the July 23 Atlas Edit spell that cut SparkLend's stablecoin borrow rate by 18 bps. Depositors reallocating out of a rate-just-cut venue is consistent with the timing. The July 12 outflow does not align with any known Sky governance action and appears unrelated to the Atlas Edit cycle.

The rest of this subsection unpacks the SparkLend stablecoin rate mechanism itself, since that is the analytical thread that runs through both the July 6 and July 23 rate events and is likely to shape SparkLend's read for months to come.

### SparkLend's stablecoin book runs on a different clock

Five of SparkLend's eighteen reserves price their borrow rate off a utilization curve whose base rate parameter is anchored to a Sky-linked rate source rather than fixed at listing. All five are stablecoins. All five stepped on July 23, by different amounts, when Sky moved that anchor. The mechanism showed up in the data that day.

At 14:43 UTC on July 23, 2026, Sky's weekly Atlas Edit executive spell executed on-chain. In that single transaction, Sky cut the Sky Savings Rate from 3.60% to 3.52%, tightened the Sky Spread from 0.10% to 0.00%, and reduced the total wholesale spread that Sky Agents pay to borrow USDS from SSR + 0.30% to SSR + 0.20%. The Sky Base Rate, which is the wholesale cost of capital that SparkLend as a Sky Agent pays for its USDS inventory, dropped from 3.90% to 3.72%. 18 bps, one transaction, one governance decision.

SparkLend's on-chain USDC borrow rate followed the same day. The retail rate stepped from 4.42% to 4.28% at the same execution, closing July at 4.28% after a full-month decline of 33 bps. The earlier step, on July 6, cut USDC 19.6 bps from 4.61% to 4.42%. The Sky Base Rate did not move on July 6; it held flat at 3.90% from July 1 through July 22. And SparkLend's other four Sky-linked stablecoins (DAI, USDT, USDS, PYUSD) did not step that day either. The July 6 move is therefore a SparkLend-side adjustment: a change to the local interest rate model on USDC that SparkLend layers on top of the shared Sky rate source, not a downstream reflection of Sky governance. Sky sets the wholesale rate; SparkLend adds a per-market markup on top of it. July 6 was Spark cutting its own USDC markup. July 23 was Sky cutting the wholesale rate everything is built on.

The chart makes the July 23 pass-through visible. Sky's governance decision propagated to SparkLend's user-facing rate in the same transaction cycle. SparkLend's markup over the Sky Base Rate (SparkLend USDC retail borrow minus Sky Base Rate) widened from 0.52 pp on July 22 to 0.56 pp on July 23. Sky cut the wholesale rate by 18 bps; SparkLend passed 14 bps through to its USDC retail rate and absorbed the remaining 4 bps into a slightly wider markup.

The pass-through is not USDC-specific. On-chain reads of SparkLend's eighteen reserves show that DAI, USDC, USDT, USDS, and PYUSD all point their variable-borrow interest rate model at a single Sky-linked rate source contract at address 0x57027B62. That contract references sUSDS in its bytecode and derives its rate from the Sky Base Rate. DAI and USDS share the same interest rate model contract; USDC, USDT, and PYUSD each have their own model contract that reads from the same rate source. The five Sky-linked reserves stepped by different amounts on July 23, depending on how much of the Sky Base Rate move passed through to each retail rate and how each pool's utilization moved that day. DAI and USDS both stepped 18.4 bps, tracking the 18 bps wholesale Base Rate move with utilization roughly stable. USDC stepped 13.8 bps, since SparkLend absorbed 4 bps into its local markup on USDC. PYUSD stepped 8 bps, a partial pass-through. USDT stepped 48.6 bps because its pool utilization dropped from 93.0% to 83.6% on the same day, stacking a utilization-driven move on top of the Sky-driven anchor shift. None of the other thirteen SparkLend reserves moved materially that day.

The other thirteen reserves are the collateral book: WETH, wstETH, WBTC, weETH, rETH, cbBTC, LBTC, tBTC, ezETH, rsETH, GNO, sDAI, and sUSDS. Each uses a standard Aave-shape interest rate strategy with base rate, slope 1, slope 2, and optimal utilization parameters set at listing. When utilization on these markets moves, the rate moves. When utilization holds, the rate holds. They are ordinary money markets.

SparkLend is a hybrid. Its Sky-linked stablecoin reserves use Aave-shape utilization curves whose base rate parameter is anchored to a Sky-linked rate source that Sky governance can move via Atlas Edit executive spells. Its collateral book of WETH, wstETH, WBTC, and the rest uses standard Aave-shape utilization curves with base rate parameters fixed at listing. On the collateral side, utilization is the whole input. On the Sky-linked stablecoin side, utilization is one of two inputs; the other is Sky governance.

Aave V3, Morpho V1, Fluid, Compound V3, and Euler V2 set their USDC IRM base rate at listing and revisit it rarely, so their rates move with utilization alone. SparkLend's collateral book follows the same convention. Its Sky-linked stablecoin reserves add a second input: the base rate anchor moves whenever Sky's Atlas Edit adjusts the Sky Base Rate. Comparing Fluid's 4.66% supply APY on USDC to SparkLend's 3.47% supply APY on USDC is influenced by both pool utilization and, in SparkLend's case, that Sky-governance-set anchor. SparkLend's rate can shift on days when its pool utilization has not moved, like July 23, in ways the other four protocols' rates cannot.

### What August tests

Whether the July 12 outflow event has a Sky-side or SparkLend-side cause that becomes visible after the fact. Whether depositor reallocation out of SparkLend following rate cuts continues or stabilises. Whether Sky governance runs another Atlas Edit adjustment to the Base Rate margin that would move SparkLend's stablecoin rates again. Whether SparkLend's USDC borrow rate holds at the July 4.28% close or drifts further with subsequent Sky governance moves.
