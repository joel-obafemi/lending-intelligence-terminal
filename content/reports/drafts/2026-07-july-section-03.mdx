<SectionHeading number="03" title="Where the Money Went" />

The July flow map has one dominant venue, one clear loser, and four protocols that broadly held or added modest ground. The full picture, in constant-price net deposit flow across the month:

- Aave V3: **+$649M**
- Morpho V1: +$135M
- Fluid: +$26M
- Compound V3: −$23M
- Euler V2: −$31M
- SparkLend: **−$203M**

Sector total: +$553M. Aave V3 alone accounted for 117% of the sector's net inflow, with the other five protocols combining for −$96M. SparkLend was the largest single-protocol contraction of the month.

### Aave V3's composition

Aave V3's July composition mirrored June. USDC contracted by $20M across the month, a much smaller drain than June's −$162M but still a net decline in the deepest stablecoin market on the platform. The remaining +$669M came from collateral inflows across the LRT and BTC-variant reserves, with the largest additions in:

- WEETH: +$285M
- sUSDe: +$145M
- WBTC: +$124M

The observation from Issue 003 continues to hold. Aave V3's growth mechanism in this cycle is collateral supply attracted by borrow-book depth, not stablecoin depositors chasing rates. Two consecutive months of the same pattern is now a trend, not a June anomaly. Depositors adding WEETH, sUSDe, and WBTC to Aave V3 are almost certainly borrowing against that collateral, and the growth reflects demand for Aave's stablecoin borrow depth.

### SparkLend's outflow

SparkLend's July −$203M net constant-price flow was the sector's largest single-protocol contraction. The month contained two outsized single-day outflows that shape the total: July 12 (−$315M) and July 28 (−$173M). The other 29 days combined for approximately +$285M of offsetting inflows.

The July 28 outflow followed the July 23 Atlas Edit spell that cut SparkLend's stablecoin borrow rate by 18 bps. Depositors reallocating out of a rate-just-cut venue is consistent with the timing. The July 12 outflow does not align with any known Sky governance action and appears unrelated to the Atlas Edit cycle. Per-protocol coverage covers both events in the SparkLend subsection.

### The mixed middle

Morpho V1 added +$135M despite the curator layer's continued consolidation into V2 vaults through the month. The V1 protocol-level inflow is not inconsistent with the V2 curator concentration story. Depositors are net-adding to Morpho at the protocol level, and the curator layer is redistributing that TVL toward V2 vaults inside the Morpho surface. Per-protocol coverage unpacks the V1/V2 split in the Morpho subsection.

Fluid added +$26M, a near-flat outcome. Rate leadership narrowing did not cause aggregate outflows on Fluid at the protocol level, even though the +281 bps June yield premium over T-bill eroded to −107 bps by month-end. Depositors did not treat the Fluid rate compression as a reason to leave.

Compound V3 and Euler V2 each shed roughly $25M to $30M in the month, modest contractions on protocols with smaller absolute TVL bases.

### Cross-protocol read

The July flow map is not "everyone shrank." It is one protocol growing large, one protocol shedding material capital, and four protocols broadly holding. The concentration in aggregate flow toward Aave V3 sits alongside stability in the middle. That picture is more consistent with a sector in which capital is picking a winner than one in which capital is broadly leaving.

Aave V3's +$669M of collateral inflow also matters for what it says about the current use case. Depositors adding LRT and BTC-variant collateral to Aave V3 are supplying it to unlock borrow capacity, not to earn a supply APY. Aave's growth in July is a function of borrowers wanting access to Aave's stablecoin borrow depth. That is a different growth mechanism than "depositors chose Aave for its yield," and it has now shown up two months in a row.
